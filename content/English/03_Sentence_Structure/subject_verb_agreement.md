# Subject-Verb Agreement: The Harmony Rules

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Subject-verb agreement is the contract of harmony in a sentence. If the subject is singular, the verb must be singular. If the subject is plural, the verb must be plural. The trick is to spot the real subject when a crowd of modifier words tries to block your view!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **Server Crash Room**
> **Max:** "The list of database servers **is** ready."
> **Chloe:** "Great! Either Leo or his teammates **have** the passwords. Let's call them."
> **Max:** "Wait, look at this. Everyone **is** trying to access the network. The team **is** experiencing high latency."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Intervening Phrases (The Noise)
Words that come between the subject and the verb (like *of, along with, as well as, including, together with*) do **not** change the number of the subject.
- ***The box** of chocolates **is** empty.* (True subject: *box*).
- ***The lead developer**, along with her team, **has** arrived.* (True subject: *developer*).

### 2. Compound Subjects ("And")
Subjects joined by *and* generally take a plural verb.
- ***Max and Chloe** **are** debugging.*

### 3. Either/Or & Neither/Nor (The Closer Neighbor)
When subjects are joined by *or, either...or, neither...nor*, the verb agrees with the **closer subject**.
- *Neither the manager nor the **engineers** **have** the key.* (Plural *engineers* is closer).
- *Neither the engineers nor the **manager** **has** the key.* (Singular *manager* is closer).

### 4. Indefinite Pronouns (The Singular Crowd)
These pronouns are always **singular**: *everyone, someone, anyone, no one, everybody, somebody, each, either, neither*.
- ***Everyone** **is** online.*
- ***Each** of the files **has** been scanned.*

### 5. Collective Nouns (The Unit)
Nouns representing a group (*team, family, class, audience*) take a **singular verb** when acting as a single unit.
- ***The team** **runs** a test daily.*

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Finger Test" & The Proximity Rule**
> - **Finger Test**: Use your finger to cover up any phrase starting with **of**, **with**, **as well as**, or **in**. What remains is your true subject!
>   * *The **leader** [of the rebels] **is** here.*
> - **The Neighbor Rule**: For *either/or* and *neither/nor*, the verb has short eyesight. It only looks at the noun directly next to it to decide singular or plural!

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- Is the word "everybody" singular or plural in agreement? :: Singular (e.g., "Everybody is ready").
- What is the agreement rule for "neither... nor" structures? :: The verb agrees with the closer subject.
- Correct this: "The cost of repairs were high." :: "The cost of repairs was high." (Subject is the singular 'cost', not 'repairs').

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"Neither of them are coming."*
> - **The Fix:** *"Neither of them **is** coming."*
> - **Why?:** *Neither* (and *either*) as a pronoun means "not one of the two," which is grammatically singular and requires a singular verb (*is*).
