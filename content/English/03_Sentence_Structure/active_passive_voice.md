# Active & Passive Voice: The Spotlight Effect

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Think of **active** and **passive** voice as moving a spotlight on a stage. In **active voice**, the spotlight shines on the character doing the action (the Subject). In **passive voice**, the spotlight shifts to the person or object receiving the action (the Object). It's all about who or what gets the attention!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **The Museum Heist**
> **Reporter Max:** "Breaking news! A local thief **stole** the museum's famous painting last night."
> **Curator Aria:** "This is terrible! The painting **was stolen** from its display room. Thankfully, a suspect **was arrested** by the police an hour ago."
> **Reporter Max:** "Has the painting **been recovered** yet?"
> **Curator Aria:** "Yes, it **is being examined** by experts right now."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

`[Object of Active Sentence] + [Be (conjugated to tense)] + [Past Participle (V3)] + [by + Agent (optional)]`

### Tense Transformation Matrix

| Tense | Active Voice (Subject does it) | Passive Voice (Subject receives it) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple** | *She writes letters.* | *Letters **are written** (by her).* |
| **Present Continuous** | *She is writing letters.* | *Letters **are being written**.* |
| **Past Simple** | *She wrote letters.* | *Letters **were written**.* |
| **Past Continuous** | *She was writing letters.* | *Letters **were being written**.* |
| **Present Perfect** | *She has written letters.* | *Letters **have been written**.* |
| **Past Perfect** | *She had written letters.* | *Letters **had been written**.* |
| **Future (Will)** | *She will write letters.* | *Letters **will be written**.* |
| **Modals** | *She must write letters.* | *Letters **must be written**.* |

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Zombie Test"**
> If you want to check if a sentence is in the passive voice, look at the verb and ask: **Can I add "by zombies" after it?**
> - *The project was completed... [by zombies]* $\rightarrow$ Grammatically works! **Passive**.
> - *We launched the app... [by zombies]* $\rightarrow$ Doesn't make grammatical sense there! **Active**.
> - *My keys have been stolen... [by zombies]* $\rightarrow$ Works! **Passive**.

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- Can the verb "happen" be passive (e.g., "The accident was happened")? :: No, because 'happen' is intransitive (it takes no object) and cannot be passive.
- Convert this active sentence to passive: "They are building a website." :: "A website is being built."
- Correct this: "The software has updated." :: "The software has been updated." (Without 'been', the software is the actor doing the updating).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"My keys have stolen."*
> - **The Fix:** *"My keys **have been stolen**."*
> - **Why?:** Active *have stolen* means the keys themselves went out and stole something. To show that someone stole the keys, you must include the passive helper verb **been** (*have been stolen*).
