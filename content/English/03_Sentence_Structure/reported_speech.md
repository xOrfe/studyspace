# Reported Speech: The Gossip Protocol

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Use **reported speech** when you are passing on information that someone else said. Instead of quoting them word-for-word, you translate their statement into the past tense, shift pronouns, and update time/place references. It is the language of reporting, journalism, and storytelling!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **The Office Rumor Mill**
> **Max:** "I **will finish** the project **tomorrow**."
> **Chloe:** (Later, to Leo) "Max said that he **would finish** the project **the next day**."
> **Max:** (To Chloe) "Do you know where Leo **is**?"
> **Chloe:** (Later, to Leo) "Max asked me if I **knew** where you **were**."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Tense Shifts (The Backshift Matrix)
When the reporting verb is in the past (*said, told, asked*), the verb in the reported clause steps back in time:

| Direct Speech | Reported Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple** (*"I code"* ) | **Past Simple** (*He said he coded*) |
| **Present Continuous** (*"I am coding"*) | **Past Continuous** (*He said he was coding*) |
| **Past Simple** (*"I coded"*) | **Past Perfect** (*He said he had coded*) |
| **Present Perfect** (*"I have coded"*) | **Past Perfect** (*He said he had coded*) |
| **Future (Will)** (*"I will code"*) | **Would** (*He said he would code*) |
| **Can** (*"I can code"*) | **Could** (*He said he could code*) |
| **Must / Have to** (*"I must code"*) | **Had to** (*He said he had to code*) |

*Note: Past Perfect, should, could, would, and might do not shift back.*

### 2. Time & Place Shifts
- *now* $\rightarrow$ **then / at that moment**
- *today* $\rightarrow$ **that day**
- *yesterday* $\rightarrow$ **the day before / the previous day**
- *tomorrow* $\rightarrow$ **the next day / the following day**
- *here / this* $\rightarrow$ **there / that**

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Step Back in Time" Rule & The Question Hack**
> - **Time Travel**: Imagine the speaker is in the past. To report their words now, you must pull all verbs **one step backward** into the past.
> - **The Question Hack**: Reported questions are NOT questions anymore—they are statements. Drop *do*, *does*, and *did*, and put the subject before the verb!
>   * *Direct:* "Where **are you** going?" $\rightarrow$ *Reported:* "She asked where **I was** going."

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What is the reporting difference between "say" and "tell"? :: 'Say' is used without a personal object (e.g., "He said he was leaving"), while 'tell' must have a personal object (e.g., "He told me he was leaving").
- Convert this to reported speech: "Don't touch the button!" :: He told me not to touch the button.
- Correct this: "She asked me where did I buy my phone." :: "She asked me where I bought my phone." (Do not use 'did' in reported questions).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"She asked me where was the keys."*
> - **The Fix:** *"She asked me where the keys **were**."*
> - **Why?:** Indirect questions must use standard statement word order: **Subject + Verb** (*where the keys were*), not question order (*where were the keys*).
