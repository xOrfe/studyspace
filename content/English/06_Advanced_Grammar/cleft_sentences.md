# Cleft Sentences: The Highlighter Pen

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> The word **cleft** means split. A cleft sentence splits a single idea into two clauses so you can run a virtual **highlighter pen** over the most important detail (the subject, object, time, or action). It redirects the reader's attention to exactly what matters most!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **Who Broke the Build?**
> **Max:** "Leo broke the server yesterday."
> **Aria:** "No, **it was Leo who** broke the database server, but **it was today that** the site went completely offline."
> **Max:** "**What we need is** a rollback script. **All I want is** to get the landing page running."
> **Aria:** "Exactly. **What he did was** run the update query without checking the backups!"

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. It-Clefts (Highlighting Nouns, People, or Times)
Used to contrast one detail against another.
`It + [is / was] + [Highlighted Element] + [that / who] + [Rest of the Sentence]`

*Standard sentence:* "Max bought the wrong server license in April."
- **Highlighting the Subject (Max):**
  * ***It was Max who** bought the wrong server license in April.* (Max, not Leo).
- **Highlighting the Object (License):**
  * ***It was the wrong server license that** Max bought in April.* (Not the database license).
- **Highlighting the Time (In April):**
  * ***It was in April that** Max bought the wrong server license.* (April, not May).

### 2. Wh-Clefts / Pseudo-Clefts (Highlighting Actions or Desires)
Used to focus on what someone did, wants, or needs.
`[What-clause] + [is / was] + [Highlighted Element]`

- **Highlighting a Desire:**
  * *Standard:* "I want a holiday." $\rightarrow$ ***What I want is** a holiday.*
- **Highlighting an Action:**
  * *Standard:* "They deleted the files." $\rightarrow$ ***What they did was** delete the files.*
- **Highlighting a Reason:**
  * *Standard:* "I came to help." $\rightarrow$ ***Why I came was** to help.*

### 3. All-Clefts (The "Only" Emphasis)
Similar to Wh-clefts, but using "All" to mean "The only thing".
- ***All I need is** your signature.* (= The only thing I need).

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Divide and Highlight" Split & The Cleft-That Rule**
> - **The Split**: Imagine snapping a pencil in half.
>   * *Pencil*: "He wanted money."
>   * *Snapped*: "What he wanted [Half 1] + was [Glue] + money [Half 2]."
> - **The "Cleft-That" Rule**: In `It-clefts`, you must use **that** (or *who* for people) even if the highlighted element is a place or time!
>   * ❌ *It was in Paris where we met.*
>   * 🔍 *It was in Paris **that** we met.*

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What does the word "cleft" mean? :: split / divided
- Convert this to a Wh-cleft emphasizing the action: "She called the police." :: "What she did was call the police."
- Correct this: "It was last winter when I learned to ski." :: "It was last winter that I learned to ski." (Use 'that' in It-clefts even for time references).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"What I hate is when people don't write tests."*
> - **The Fix:** *"What I hate **is people not writing tests** / **that people don't write tests**."*
> - **Why?:** A Wh-cleft starting with *What I hate* expects a noun phrase or a *that-clause* as its complement. Placing an adverbial time clause (*when people don't write tests*) after *is/was* is grammatically mismatched.
