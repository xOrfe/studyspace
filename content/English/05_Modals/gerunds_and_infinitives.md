# Gerunds & Infinitives: The Verb Matchmaker

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> What happens when two verbs meet in a sentence? The first verb acts as a matchmaker, dictating the grammatical outfit of the second verb! It will either force the second verb into a **Gerund** (Verb + -ing) or an **Infinitive** (to + Verb). Learn the matchmaking rules to avoid awkward grammatical combinations!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **Learning Rust**
> **Max:** "I decided **to learn** Rust. I want **to write** faster code."
> **Aria:** "That's great! You should avoid **copying** code from tutorials. I recommend **reading** the official documentation."
> **Max:** "I remember **reading** it once, but I forgot **to bookmark** the page. Let me try **searching** for it now."
> **Aria:** "Thanks for **sharing** the link when you find it!"

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. The Gerund Club (Verbs + V-ing)
These verbs must be paired with a Gerund: *avoid, enjoy, finish, imagine, keep, mind, postpone, recommend, suggest, spend time*.
- *I enjoy **coding** in Python.*
- *She suggested **buying** a new monitor.*

### 2. The Infinitive Club (Verbs + to-Verb)
These verbs must be paired with a to-Infinitive: *agree, decide, hope, learn, manage, offer, plan, promise, refuse, want, would like*.
- *We decided **to update** our database.*
- *He promised **to write** the test cases.*

### 3. The Preposition Rule (Preposition + V-ing)
Any verb following a preposition (*in, on, at, of, about, for, before, after, by*) **must** be a Gerund.
- *He is good **at designing** interfaces.*
- *Thank you **for helping** me.*

### 4. The Double Agents (Verbs with Meaning Changes)

| Verb | Followed by Infinitive (to-Verb) | Followed by Gerund (V-ing) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **remember** | Remember a duty first, then do it.<br>*I remembered to lock the door.* | Have a memory of a past action.<br>*I remember locking the door.* |
| **forget** | Forget a duty or task you had to do.<br>*I forgot to call the client.* | Forget a past experience (usually negative).<br>*I'll never forget meeting you.* |
| **stop** | Pause an action in order to start another.<br>*I stopped to smoke.* (Stopped walking to smoke). | Cease or quit an ongoing activity.<br>*I stopped smoking.* (Quit the habit). |
| **try** | Make a physical/mental effort to do something.<br>*Try to open the stuck window.* | Experiment to see if it solves a problem.<br>*Try opening the window.* (To cool down). |

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Preposition Magnet" & The "Stop" Movie**
> - **Preposition Magnet**: Prepositions are like magnetic poles that attract the **-ing** suffix. Whenever you see *of, in, at, for, by, about* followed by a verb, slap that *-ing* on immediately!
>   * *Exception Alert:* The phrase **look forward to** ends with a prepositional `to`. Therefore, it takes a Gerund: *I look forward to **meeting** you.*
> - **The Stop Test**: Picture a walking person. *Stop to do* means they halt to perform a new action. *Stop doing* means they are in the middle of an action and freeze/quit it.

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What is the difference between "I remembered to buy milk" and "I remembered buying milk"? :: "I remembered to buy milk" means I remembered my duty and then bought it. "I remembered buying milk" means I have a visual memory of purchasing milk in the past.
- Which form follows the verb "enjoy"? :: Gerund (e.g., "I enjoy reading").
- Correct this: "I am interested in to learn Spanish." :: "I am interested in learning Spanish." (Preposition 'in' must be followed by a gerund).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"I am looking forward to meet you next week."*
> - **The Fix:** *"I am looking forward to **meeting** you next week."*
> - **Why?:** In *look forward to*, the word *to* is a preposition, not the marker of an infinitive. Like all prepositions, it must be followed by a Gerund (*meeting*).
