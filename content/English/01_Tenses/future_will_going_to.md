# Future Forms: Will vs. Going to

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Use **will** for spontaneous, heat-of-the-moment decisions, promises, and speculative guesses. Use **be going to** for pre-planned intentions and predictions backed by clear, present evidence. Think of **will** as the split-second reflex and **going to** as the blueprints already in your hand!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **The Dinner Disaster**
> **Ben:** "Oh no! We're completely out of milk for the coffee."
> **Chloe:** "Don't worry, I **will run** to the corner store and grab some."
> **Ben:** "Wait, look at those black storm clouds! It **is going to pour** any second."
> **Chloe:** "I know, that's why I **am going to take** my umbrella."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Will (Modal Auxiliary)
`[Subject] + [will / 'll] + [Verb (Base Form)]`
- **Positive (+):** *I **will help** you with those bags.* / *We **will finish** this later.*
- **Negative (-):** *She **will not (won't) attend** the meeting.*
- **Question (?):** ***Will** you **marry** me?*

### 2. Be Going To
`[Subject] + [am / is / are] + [going to] + [Verb (Base Form)]`
- **Positive (+):** *We **are going to build** a new website.* / *I **am going to paint** my room.*
- **Negative (-):** *He **is not (isn't) going to buy** that car.*
- **Question (?):** ***Are** they **going to join** us?*

---

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Spark vs. Track" Rule**
> - **Will** is the **Spark**: Sudden, immediate, no preparation (*The phone rings! I'll get it!*).
> - **Going to** is the **Track**: You are already on the path, the decision was made before (*I've got the tickets! I'm going to see the concert.*).
> - **Evidence prediction**: If your eyes see it happening *right now* (like a vase tipping over or dark clouds), use **going to**!

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What is the negative contraction of "will"? :: won't
- When do we use "going to" for predictions? :: When there is present, visible evidence (e.g., "Look at those clouds, it's going to rain").
- Correct this: "I will meet my boss at 10 AM tomorrow." :: "I am going to meet my boss / I am meeting my boss at 10 AM tomorrow." (It is a pre-arranged schedule/plan).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"I think they are going to win the match next year."*
> - **The Fix:** *"I think they **will win** the match next year."*
> - **Why?:** For general speculation about the far future, especially after *I think*, *I believe*, or *I hope*, use **will** unless you have immediate, physical evidence.
