# Present Perfect Continuous: The Marathon Tense

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Use this tense to show the **duration of an action** that started in the past and is still going on, or has just finished with a visible physical result. It answers the question: **"How long?"**

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **Meeting a Friend in the Rain**
> **Avery:** "Oh my god, you are soaking wet! **Have you been running** in the rain?"
> **Liam:** "Yes! I **have been trying** to find a taxi for 40 minutes, but none of them are stopping!"
> **Avery:** "Come inside. I **have been making** tea, it's nice and hot."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)
How to build it:

`[Subject] + [have / has] + [been] + [Verb + -ing]`

- **Positive (+):** *She **has been reading** that novel all afternoon.*
- **Negative (-):** *They **have not (haven't) been sleeping** well lately.*
- **Question (?):** ***Have** you **been waiting** long?*

---

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **Process vs. Product**
> To understand the difference between Present Perfect Simple and Continuous:
> - **Continuous (Process - "How long"):** Focuses on the activity itself and the time spent.
>   * *I **have been painting** the living room.* (My clothes are covered in paint; the activity was ongoing).
> - **Simple (Product - "How many"):** Focuses on completion and the final count.
>   * *I **have painted** two walls.* (They are finished; look at the result!).

---

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- Can we use Present Perfect Continuous with stative verbs like "know" or "like"? :: No, stative verbs can never be used in continuous forms. Use Present Perfect Simple instead (e.g., "I've known him for years").
- Correct this: "I've been having this phone for six months." :: "I've had this phone for six months." (Have/possession is stative).
- What does it mean if I say, "I've been working here for ten years"? :: It means I started working here ten years ago and I still work here today.

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"I am living in London since 2020."*
> - **The Fix:** *"I **have been living** in London since 2020."*
> - **Why?:** Present Continuous (*I am living*) cannot be paired with a duration marker like *since* or *for*. You must use the Present Perfect Continuous to bridge the past to the present!
