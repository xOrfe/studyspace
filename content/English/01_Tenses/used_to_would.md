# Used to & Would: The Nostalgia Tense

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Use **used to** and **would** to talk about past habits, routines, or states that are **no longer true today**. They are perfect for sharing memories, comparing the past with the present, and expressing nostalgia.

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **Grandpa Reminiscing**
> **Grandpa:** "When I was young, we **didn't use to have** smartphones. We **used to play** outside until the streetlights came on."
> **Grandkid:** "Did you get bored?"
> **Grandpa:** "Never! Every Saturday, my friends and I **would ride** our bikes to the lake, and we **would spend** hours fishing."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Used To (Habits & States)
Can be used for both **past actions** and **past states** (e.g., feelings, locations, possession).
- **Positive (+):** *I **used to live** in a small apartment.* (State $\rightarrow$ OK!)
- **Negative (-):** *I **didn't use to like** olives.* (Note: *use to*, not *used to* after *didn't*!)
- **Question (?):** ***Did** you **use to play** the piano?*

### 2. Would (Actions Only!)
Can **only** be used for **repeated past actions** (habits/routines). Cannot be used for past states.
- **Positive (+):** *Every Sunday, my dad **would make** pancakes.* (Action $\rightarrow$ OK!)
- **Negative (-):** *He **wouldn't play** with us unless we asked.*
- **State restriction:** ❌ *I **would have** long hair.* (Incorrect! Having hair is a state. Use: *I **used to have** long hair*).

---

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Action vs. State" Test**
> If you can physically act out the verb like a mime (e.g., *run, cook, ride, speak*), you can use both **used to** and **would**.
> If you cannot mime it because it's a state of being (e.g., *be, live, own, like, know*), you **must** use **used to**!
> - 🏃 *I **used to go** to gym* / *I **would go** to gym.* (Mimeable $\rightarrow$ Both OK).
> - 🧠 *I **used to believe** in Santa.* (Not mimeable $\rightarrow$ Only *used to*).

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- Can you say "I would live in London"? :: No, living is a state. You must say "I used to live in London."
- What is the negative spelling of "used to"? :: didn't use to (without the 'd').
- Correct this: "I would be shy when I was a child." :: "I used to be shy when I was a child." (Being shy is a state).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"I am used to wake up early."* (When meaning "I did this in the past").
> - **The Fix:** *"I **used to wake** up early."*
> - **Why?:** *Be used to + -ing* means "to be accustomed to something now." *Used to + base verb* means "a past habit." Don't mix them up!
