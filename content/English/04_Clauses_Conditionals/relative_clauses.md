# Relative Clauses: The Detail Injector

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Think of a **relative clause** as a surgical detail injector. Instead of writing two separate, choppy sentences, you inject a pronoun like *who*, *which*, *that*, or *whose* to blend them together and specify exactly which noun you are talking about!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **The Missing Device**
> **Max:** "Where is the laptop **that** was on the desk?"
> **Aria:** "Leo, **who** is always borrowing things, took it to the conference room **where** the meeting is happening."
> **Max:** "Oh, he is the developer **whose** code broke the production server last week!"
> **Aria:** "Yes! That is the reason **why** he needs it!"

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### Pronoun & Adverb Matching Matrix

| Pronoun / Adverb | Replaced Target | In-Context Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **who** | People (Subject) | *The engineer **who** built the system is a genius.* |
| **whom** | People (Object - Formal) | *The client **whom** we met yesterday signed the contract.* |
| **which** | Things & Animals | *The server **which** crashed has been rebooted.* |
| **that** | People & Things (Informal) | *The code **that** I wrote works perfectly.* |
| **whose** | Possession (Ownership) | *The designer **whose** mockups were selected is celebrating.* |
| **where** | Places | *This is the office **where** we work.* |
| **when** | Times | *I remember the night **when** we launched.* |
| **why** | Reasons | *That is the reason **why** the build failed.* |

## Defining vs. Non-Defining relative Clauses

### 1. Defining relative Clauses (Essential Details)
- Provide essential information needed to identify the noun.
- **No commas** are used.
- *that* can replace *who* or *which*.
- **The Omission Rule**: You can delete the pronoun if it is followed by a **Subject + Verb** (meaning it acts as the object):
  * *The book **[that]** I bought...* $\rightarrow$ (Omission OK!)
  * *The boy **who** called you...* $\rightarrow$ (No omission! Pronoun is followed by a verb).

### 2. Non-Defining relative Clauses (Extra Details)
- Provide parenthetical, non-essential information.
- **Must be separated by commas**.
- *that* **cannot** be used (only *who* or *which*).
- You **cannot** omit the pronoun.
- *My MacBook, **which** I bought in 2024, is running slowly.* (Commas are required; cannot say *My MacBook, that I bought...*).

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Comma-That Shield" & The Verb Test**
> - **The Shield**: Commas and the word `that` are like fire and water—they cannot mix! If there are commas around your relative clause, the word `that` is strictly banned. Use `who` or `which` instead.
> - **The Verb Test**: If the word immediately following your relative pronoun is a **Verb**, do NOT delete the pronoun! (*"The girl **who runs** the department"* $\rightarrow$ *who* is the subject of the clause and must stay).

---

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- Can you use "that" in a non-defining relative clause (with commas)? :: No, 'that' is banned in non-defining clauses.
- When are you allowed to omit the relative pronoun in a defining clause? :: When the pronoun acts as the object of the clause (meaning it is followed by a Subject + Verb).
- Correct this: "The designer which drew the logo won an award." :: "The designer who / that drew the logo won an award." (Use 'who/that' for people, not 'which').

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"This is the website which I built it last night."*
> - **The Fix:** *"This is the website which I **built** last night."*
> - **Why?:** Do not double-refer to the object! The relative pronoun *which* already represents the website, so adding the pronoun *it* at the end is redundant.
