# Wish Clauses: The Regret & Desire Machine

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Think of **wish** and **if only** as a magic genie. But there is a catch: the genie only changes realities in your mind! Use wish clauses to express present dissatisfaction, past regrets, or complaints about future situations. It's the ultimate grammar of "what could have been!"

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **The Slow Compile**
> **Max:** "I wish I **had** a faster CPU. This compilation is taking forever."
> **Chloe:** "If only we **hadn't bought** the cheap hardware last month!"
> **Max:** "Yeah. And I wish it **would stop** making that loud buzzing sound! It's driving me crazy."
> **Chloe:** "I wish I **could** help you, but I hope you **finish** soon so we can leave."

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Present Wishes (Present Dissatisfaction)
Expressing a desire for a present situation to be different from reality.
`Subject + wish + [Past Simple]  /  If only + [Past Simple]`
- *I wish I **spoke** fluent Japanese.* (Reality: I don't speak it).
- *If only he **were** here.* (Reality: He is absent).
- *Grammar Hack:* Use **were** instead of **was** for all singular subjects (*I wish I were*, *I wish it were*).

### 2. Past Wishes (Past Regrets)
Expressing regret about a past event that cannot be changed.
`Subject + wish + [Past Perfect (had + V3)]  /  If only + [Past Perfect]`
- *I wish I **had studied** computer science in college.* (Reality: I studied history).
- *If only we **hadn't missed** the bus.* (Reality: We missed it).

### 3. Future Wishes & Complaints (Annoyance / Desired Change)
Expressing impatience, annoyance, or a desire for someone else's behavior (or a situation) to change.
`Subject + wish + [different Subject] + [would + Verb (Base Form)]`
- *I wish you **would stop** tapping your pen!* (Complaint about a habit).
- *I wish the rain **would clear** up.* (Desire for situation to change).
- *Warning:* You cannot use *would* for yourself (*"I wish I would..."*). Use *could* or *hope* instead.

---

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Grammatical Time Machine" & The "Self-Would" Ban**
> - **Tense Shift**: The genie always pushes the tense **one step backward** into the past:
>   * Want a different present? $\rightarrow$ Slide to **Past Simple** (*wish I had*).
>   * Want a different past? $\rightarrow$ Slide to **Past Perfect** (*wish I had had*).
>   * Want a different future? $\rightarrow$ Slide to **Would** (*wish they would*).
> - **Self-Would Ban**: You cannot use `would` for yourself because you have control over your own actions! 
>   * ❌ *I wish I would pass.* $\rightarrow$ 🔍 *I **hope** I pass.* / *I wish I **could** pass.*

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What tense is used to express a regret about a past event? :: Past Perfect (had + V3)
- What is the main difference between "wish" and "hope"? :: "Wish" is for unreal, impossible, or highly unlikely things; "hope" is for realistic, possible future events.
- Correct this: "I wish I didn't lose my passport yesterday." :: "I wish I hadn't lost my passport yesterday." (Past regrets require the Past Perfect).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"I wish I would be taller."*
> - **The Fix:** *"I wish I **were** taller."*
> - **Why?:** Do not use *would* to express a wish about a state that you cannot change (like height). *Would* is strictly for actions, behaviors, or temporary situations you want to change (usually of other people).
