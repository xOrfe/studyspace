# Conditionals Type 0, 1 & 2: The Multi-Verse Choices

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Think of conditionals as portals to different dimensions of reality. **Type 0** is the Dimension of Facts (always true). **Type 1** is the Dimension of Probabilities (realistic future plans). **Type 2** is the Dimension of Dreams (completely imaginary present or future). Choose your portal wisely!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **Daydreaming in class**
> **Max:** "If we **heat** ice, it **melts**. Why does physics have to be so dry?"
> **Chloe:** "Well, if it **rains** tomorrow, we **will skip** class and watch movies."
> **Max:** "But what if we **won** the lottery right now? I **would buy** a massive server farm."
> **Chloe:** "If I **were** you, I **would buy** a tropical island instead!"

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Type 0 (General Truths & Science)
Used for things that are always true under certain conditions.
`If + [Present Simple] , + [Present Simple]`
- *If you **mix** blue and yellow, you **get** green.*
- *If I **drink** coffee late at night, I **cannot** sleep.*

### 2. Type 1 (Real & Realistic Futures)
Used for likely future scenarios and their direct consequences.
`If + [Present Simple] , + [will / won't + Verb (Base Form)]`
- *If it **rains** tomorrow, we **will cancel** the picnic.*
- *If you **study** hard, you **will pass** the exam.*

### 3. Type 2 (Unreal & Imaginary Presents/Futures)
Used for hypothetical, impossible, or highly unlikely situations.
`If + [Past Simple] , + [would / wouldn't + Verb (Base Form)]`
- *If I **had** a rocket, I **would fly** to Mars.* (I don't have a rocket).
- *If I **were** you, I **would accept** the job offer.* (Giving advice).
- *Grammar Hack:* In Type 2, always use **were** instead of **was** for singular pronouns (*If I were, If she were, If it were*).

---

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Allergy" Rule & Subjunctive Were**
> - **The Allergy**: The *if-clause* is strictly **allergic** to *will* or *would*. Keep them in the result clause only!
>   * ❌ *If I will see him, I will tell him.* $\rightarrow$ 🔍 *If I **see** him, I will tell him.*
>   * ❌ *If I would be rich, I would travel.* $\rightarrow$ 🔍 *If I **were** rich, I would travel.*
> - **The "Unreal Were"**: Think of "were" as the magic wand that changes the real world into a fantasy world (*"If he **were** here..."* $\rightarrow$ but he isn't!).

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What does "unless" stand for? :: "if not" (e.g., "Unless you hurry, you'll be late" = "If you don't hurry, you'll be late").
- Can you put "will" inside the if-clause of a Type 1 conditional? :: No, use the Present Simple in the if-clause.
- Correct this: "If I was you, I would take the offer." :: "If I were you, I would take the offer." (Use 'were' for hypothetical advice).

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"If I would have more time, I would learn Spanish."*
> - **The Fix:** *"If I **had** more time, I would learn Spanish."*
> - **Why?:** Do not double-use *would* in both clauses! The condition clause (*if...*) must take the Past Simple in Type 2, while only the result clause takes *would*.
