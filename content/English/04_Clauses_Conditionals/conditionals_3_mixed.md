# Conditionals Type 3 & Mixed: The Time Machine & Regrets

> [!abstract] **The Concept in a Nutshell**
> Step into the realm of time travel! **Type 3** allows you to change the past in your imagination, usually to express regrets or evaluate history. **Mixed Conditionals** act like time wormholes, linking a past event to a present outcome, or a permanent trait to a past choice. Let's rewrite history!

---

## Scene Study (Real-World Dialogue)
> [!example] **The Post-Mortem Meeting**
> **Max:** "If we **had checked** the logs yesterday, we **would have spotted** the database leak."
> **Chloe:** "I know. And if we **had fixed** it then, our website **would be** online right now instead of being crashed."
> **Max:** "Well, if you **were** a more careful developer, you **would have noticed** the error anyway!"
> **Chloe:** "Hey! If I **weren't** a patient colleague, I **would have walked** out of this room an hour ago!"

---

## The Blueprint (Form & Structure)

### 1. Type 3 (Unreal Past & Regrets)
Imagining a different past action and its past result. Both condition and result are in the past.
`If + [Past Perfect (had + V3)] , + [would have + Past Participle (V3)]`
- *If they **had left** earlier, they **would have caught** the train.* (But they left late and missed it).
- *If I **hadn't spent** all my money, I **would have bought** the course.*

### 2. Mixed Conditionals (Time Wormholes)

#### **Type A: Past Action $\rightarrow$ Present State**
How an imaginary past event affects the present moment.
`If + [Past Perfect (had + V3)] , + [would + Verb (Base Form)]`
- *If I **had taken** that job last year, I **would live** in London now.* (Condition: past; Result: present state).
- *If she **hadn't missed** the flight, she **would be** here with us.*

#### **Type B: Permanent Trait $\rightarrow$ Past Action**
How an ongoing quality or permanent state affected a past action.
`If + [Past Simple] , + [would have + Past Participle (V3)]`
- *If I **spoke** French, I **would have helped** that tourist yesterday.* (I don't speak French as a general rule).
- *If she **were** organized, she **would not have lost** the keys.*

## Mnemonic & Memory Hacks
> [!tip] **The "Double Had/Have" & The Inversion Leap**
> - **Double Past**: Type 3 is **pure double past**. You must have one `had` in the condition house and one `have` in the result house: `If I HAD checked... I would HAVE seen...`
> - **The Inversion Leap**: Drop the word `if` and leap `had` to the front of the sentence. It sounds highly sophisticated:
>   * *If I had known...* $\rightarrow$ ***Had I** known...*
>   * *If it hadn't been for...* $\rightarrow$ ***Had it** not been for...*

## Interactive Flashcards (Spaced Repetition)
- What is the structure of a Type 3 conditional if-clause? :: Past Perfect (had + V3)
- Invert this clause: "If they had asked me..." :: "Had they asked me..."
- Correct this: "If I had won the lottery yesterday, I would have been rich now." :: "If I had won the lottery yesterday, I would be rich now." (The result is in the present 'now', so use 'would be').

---

## The Trap (Common Mistakes)
> [!danger] **Avoid the Blunder!**
> - **The Mistake:** *"If I would have studied history, I would have passed the exam."*
> - **The Fix:** *"If I **had studied** history, I would have passed the exam."*
> - **Why?:** Never put *would* or *would have* inside the *if-clause*. The condition clause must take the past helper verb *had*.
